# Thrift+ Legal Research Memo

Eco-Thrift LLC, Omaha, Nebraska · Sep 30, 2026 · @Bill Rollins

## How to read this

This is research, not legal advice. It is written for Eco-Thrift's attorney to check before the Oct 20, 2026 launch of Thrift+.

Each question gets four parts: **Answer**, **Basis** (statutes, rules, agency guidance you can look up), **Confidence** (High, Medium, Low), and **Verify** (what the attorney should confirm). Where an answer turns on a fact not given, it says which fact.

Facts assumed throughout, unless a question says otherwise: Eco-Thrift sells only in person in Omaha; it is a small business by SBA size standards; "adult items" means sealed sexual novelties or adult media, not tobacco, vapes, hemp or THC products, or kratom.

## A. ID checks and stored data

The ID process is lawful as designed. The one real trap is adding an ID scanner later.

### 1. Looking at an ID and storing only a flag, first name and phone

**Answer:** Yes, this is lawful, and § 60-4,111.01 doesn't reach it. That statute governs only "machine-readable information encoded on" a license or state ID (the barcode and magnetic stripe). Looking at the card and typing a first name and an 18+ flag never touches that data.

The age-verification exception doesn't help you, and you don't need it. It covers only retailers licensed to sell alcohol, lottery tickets or tobacco, letting them scan and store age and ID number with a posted sign. Eco-Thrift holds none of those licenses.

So if you ever add a scanner for age checks, you would be outside the exception. Storing scanned data outside the exceptions is a Class IV felony. Don't add one without counsel.

**Basis:**

- [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-4,111.01(2)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-4%2C111.01): no person with access to the machine-readable information may "compile, store, preserve, trade, sell, or share" it.
- § 60-4,111.01(3)(a) and (3)(e): the age exception for licensed alcohol, lottery and tobacco retailers, with a sign requirement and an 18-month storage cap.
- § 60-4,111.01(4): a narrow exception for approving checks, electronic transfers and similar payments, and for fraud prevention.
- § 60-4,111.01(3)(d) and (5), with § 28-105: Class IV felony, up to 2 years and $10,000.

**Confidence:** High on the statute's text. Medium-high that a visual check is outside it; no court or AG opinion addresses that.

**Verify:** That no case or AG opinion reads the statute more broadly. Whether a scanner that reads age and stores nothing would be allowed for a store without an alcohol, lottery or tobacco license.

### 2. Remaining obligations if you never scan or copy the ID

**Answer:** None that I found. Nebraska requires ID-check signage only when a card is scanned. No statute requires consent or disclosure for a visual check.

What does apply is the general rule that what you tell customers must be true. If the terms say you never copy IDs, an employee photographing one on a phone turns that statement into a deceptive practice.

Recommended anyway: one line at the register and in the terms, such as "We look at your ID to confirm your name and that you're 18 or older. We don't scan, copy or keep it." Train staff never to type ID numbers, birth dates or addresses into any field, including notes.

**Basis:** § 60-4,111.01(3)(a) and (b) (signs required only for scanning); [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 59-1602](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=59-1602) (unfair or deceptive acts); FTC Act § 5, 15 U.S.C. § 45.

**Confidence:** Medium. This is a finding that no rule exists, which is harder to prove than one that does.

**Verify:** The Omaha Municipal Code for any local ID or signage rule.

### 3. How long to keep the 18+ flag, and removal on request

**Answer:** No law sets a retention period for the flag, and none requires removing it on request, as long as Eco-Thrift stays exempt from the Nebraska Data Privacy Act (Q6). The flag is also not "personal information" under the breach-notice law.

Build two things anyway. Keep the flag only while the membership is active, and let a customer close the account and have everything deleted. Once your terms promise deletion, the promise is enforceable under the deceptive-practices laws.

**Basis:** NDPA small-business exemption, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-1103(1)(c); the deletion right in § 87-1107(2) binds only covered businesses; breach-law definition, § 87-802(5).

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Depends on:** Eco-Thrift remaining an SBA small business once affiliated businesses are counted (Q6).

## B. The customer photo

The photo is lawful with clear consent. Nebraska has no biometric privacy law, but the FTC treats a face photo as biometric information, so retention and security matter.

### 4. Is the register photo lawful, and what consent is needed?

**Answer:** Yes. No Nebraska law requires consent to photograph a customer for internal use, and Nebraska has no biometric privacy statute. The privacy claims that could matter are appropriation of a likeness "for advertising or commercial purposes" and intrusion into seclusion. Consent defeats both, as long as you use the photo only the way the customer agreed.

Consent is your protection, so capture it as a deliberate act. Verbal consent is legally enough, but a tap on a customer-facing screen is far easier to prove. The prompt should say what the photo is for and what you won't do: "Used only so staff can confirm it's you. Never used for marketing, never shared, never run through face recognition." No posted sign is required.

On biometrics: under the NDPA, a photo is "biometric data" only when it is used to identify someone through automatic measurement, which you never do. The FTC's 2023 policy statement goes further and counts any face photo as biometric information. It flags surprise collection, indefinite retention and weak security as possible unfair practices. It isn't binding, but it's the yardstick an investigator would use.

On minors: § 20-205 says a parent or guardian "may" consent for a minor. Nebraska's age of majority is 19, but 18-year-olds can now sign binding contracts, so the real question is members under 18. The risk for an internal photo is small, but decide on purpose whether under-18 signups get a photo.

**Basis:**

- [Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 20-202, 20-203, 20-205, 20-211](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/display_html.php?begin_section=20-201&end_section=20-211): appropriation, intrusion, the consent defense, one-year limitation period.
- Neb. Rev. Stat. § 43-2101: age of majority is 19, but 18-year-olds may make binding contracts under subsection (2).
- [NDPA § 87-1102(3)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=87-1102): biometric data requires "automatic measurement"; photographs are excluded "except when generated to identify a specific individual."
- [FTC, Policy Statement on Biometric Information and Section 5 of the FTC Act (May 18, 2023)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p225402biometricpolicystatement.pdf).
- Biometric bills not enacted: LB 954 (2024), LB 204 (2025); LB 729 (2026) withdrawn.

**Confidence:** High that it's lawful with consent. Medium on the biometric points.

**Verify:** That no biometric bill passed in 2026. Whether a photo staff use to recognize a member could be argued to be "generated to identify a specific individual" (NDPA) or an "other unique physical representation" (breach law). Whether to require a parent's consent for under-18 photos.

### 5. How long to keep the photo, and deletion requests

**Answer:** No Nebraska or federal law sets a retention period for an exempt small business, and none requires deletion on request. But the FTC says keeping biometric information "indefinitely" or without a business need raises the risk of an unfairness claim. Its orders against Drizly, Blackbaud and Rite Aid each required a written retention schedule.

Suggested rule: keep the photo while the membership is active, delete it after 24 months with no purchase, and delete within 30 days of a request. The 24 months is a business choice, not a legal number. Write the schedule down and make the software actually follow it.

For a customer who wants the photo gone, offer two options: keep the card and show ID at every age-restricted sale, or close the account. Say in the policy that deleted photos leave backups on their normal rotation, and dispose of them securely.

**Basis:** FTC Biometric Policy Statement (2023); FTC orders in Drizly (2022), Blackbaud (2024) and [Rite Aid (modified order, Docket C-4308, March 2024)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/c4308riteaidmodifiedorder.pdf), which caps retention at 5 years; [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-808(1)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=87-808) (safe disposal of covered personal information).

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Verify:** That the deletion promise matches what the software and backups actually do.

## C. Personal data and privacy

The Nebraska Data Privacy Act almost certainly doesn't apply to Eco-Thrift. Your privacy duties come from what you promise, from basic security law, and from the TCPA if you ever text.

### 6. Does the Nebraska Data Privacy Act, or any other privacy law, apply?

**Answer:** The NDPA almost certainly doesn't apply. It has no revenue or data-volume threshold. Instead it exempts any business that is a "small business" under the federal Small Business Act, and for used merchandise retailers (NAICS 459510) that line is $14.0 million in average annual receipts.

One NDPA duty does reach small businesses: you may not sell sensitive data without the consumer's prior consent. Your data isn't sensitive (photos you never measure aren't biometric data), and you don't plan to sell it.

What applies regardless of size:

- **Breach notice and security, §§ 87-801 to 87-808.** These cover "personal information": first name or initial and last name plus an SSN, license number, account number or biometric data, or a username or email plus a password. With first name only, your data likely falls outside. If the app ever logs members in with a phone number and a PIN or password, that pair could count.
- **Deceptive practices, § 59-1602 and FTC Act § 5.** Whatever your privacy notice says becomes enforceable.
- **COPPA,** only if a member app is used by children under 13 (Q21).

If you ever outgrow the exemption, the notice must list the categories of data processed, the purposes, how to exercise and appeal rights, data shared with third parties, the categories of third parties, and how to submit requests. Members would gain rights to access, correct, delete, port and opt out, answered within 45 days.

**Basis:**

- [NDPA, Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 87-1101 to 87-1130](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/display_html.php?begin_section=87-1101&end_section=87-1130): applicability § 87-1103(1)(c); small-business duty § 87-1118(1); sensitive data § 87-1102(30); notice § 87-1113; rights §§ 87-1107 to 87-1109; AG-only enforcement with 30-day notice and cure, up to $7,500 per violation, §§ 87-1119, 87-1122, 87-1124, 87-1125.
- 13 CFR 121.201, NAICS 459510 at $14.0 million ([SBA inflation adjustment, Fed. Reg. Nov. 17, 2022, FR Doc. 2022-24595](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-11-17/html/2022-24595.htm)).
- [Breach law, §§ 87-801 to 87-808](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/display_html.php?begin_section=87-801&end_section=87-808): definition § 87-802(5); notice § 87-803; security § 87-808.

**Confidence:** High, if receipts are well under $14 million.

**Depends on:** Average annual receipts counting affiliates. The SBA adds affiliates' receipts (13 CFR 121.103 and 121.104), which can include businesses under common ownership and sometimes close family members' businesses. Rollins Family LLC and your wife's business are the ones to check.

**Verify:** The affiliation analysis. Whether the NDPA's phrase "as such act existed on January 1, 2024" also freezes the SBA's size table at that date.

### 7. Written terms and privacy policy

**Answer:** No law requires either for an exempt small business, but you need both. Written terms are what make "no cash value," expiry, final-sale and ID rules binding. A short privacy notice keeps you safe under the deceptive-practices laws, because it forces you to say only what's true. One document in two parts is fine.

The terms should cover:

- **Eligibility:** minimum age (Q21), one account per person, the card is personal, staff may ask for ID.
- **How the reward is calculated:** the schedule or formula, so a member can predict tomorrow's price.
- **Instant vs banked,** the 5% bonus, the $10 monthly cover, and how they combine.
- **No cash value,** and expiry rules for banked rewards and store credit (Q13).
- **Returns:** who, how long, what qualifies, 95% store credit, exclusions, "as is" (Q12).
- **Tax:** calculated on the price after member rewards; store credit is a payment (Q15 to Q18).
- **Lost or misused cards.**
- **Changes and ending the program:** notice period, changes apply going forward, banked rewards and credit honored.
- **Privacy:** what you collect, why, who sees it, how long you keep it, how to delete it, who to contact.

Rewards that change over time need three extra commitments: disclose the method, apply changes going forward only, and honor what's already banked. Nebraska implies a covenant of good faith in every contract. It won't override an express right to change the program, but it limits a change that strips benefits members already earned.

**Basis:** § 59-1602; [§ 87-302](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=87-302) (deceptive trade practices); Spanish Oaks, Inc. v. Hy-Vee, Inc., 265 Neb. 133, 655 N.W.2d 390 (2003) (implied covenant); [Northwest, Inc. v. Ginsberg, 572 U.S. 273 (2014)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/572/273/) (frequent-flyer terms are a contract that carries the covenant under state law); [§ 43-2101(2)(a)(i)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=43-2101) (18-year-olds can sign binding contracts).

**Confidence:** High that you need them. Medium on how far the covenant limits changes.

**Verify:** Spanish Oaks and any newer Nebraska authority on the covenant; a 2025 case, Henderson State Co. v. Garrelts, 319 Neb. 485, was reported but not opened. That the change clause is drafted so the program isn't an illusory promise.

### 8. Using members' phone numbers

**Answer:** Using the number as a lookup key carries no legal restriction. The rules start when you send texts or make calls.

- **Marketing texts** sent by autodialer need prior express written consent: a signed agreement (an e-signature works) that names the number and says consent isn't a condition of purchase.
- **Informational texts** (balance, receipt) need prior express consent, a lower bar.
- **Revocation** by any reasonable means must be honored within 10 business days. The FCC's "revoke-all" piece is delayed to Jan 31, 2027, and a vote to narrow it was scheduled for today, Sep 30, 2026.
- **The one-to-one consent rule** was vacated in January 2025.
- **Damages** are $500 per text, up to $1,500 if willful. Texting class actions are common.

If texting is even possible later, add an unchecked opt-in with written-consent language at signup now. Collecting consent from every member later is much harder.

Nebraska's Automatic Dialing-Announcing Devices Act requires a Public Service Commission permit for autodialed solicitations. Whether it covers texts is unresolved.

**Basis:** 47 U.S.C. § 227(b) and (c); [47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2), (a)(10), (f)(9)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200); [Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC, No. 24-10277 (11th Cir. Jan. 24, 2025)](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202410277.pdf); [FCC Order DA 26-12 (Jan. 6, 2026)](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-12A1.pdf); Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 86-236 to 86-257 (dialing devices) and §§ 86-212 to 86-235 (telemarketing).

**Confidence:** High on federal text. Low on how Nebraska treats texts.

**Verify:** The outcome of the FCC's Sep 30, 2026 vote. Whether the Nebraska dialing-device permit covers texts, before you send any.

## D. Age-restricted items

Nebraska's under-18 rule for adult items covers media and genital-shaped devices. Crossbows have no legal minimum age, so 18+ there is your policy, not the law.

### 9. What the law requires for adult items and crossbows

**Answer, adult items:** Nebraska makes it a crime to knowingly sell, or "display for sale," to a minor any picture, film, recording, book or magazine that is harmful to minors. It also covers "any replica, article, or device having the appearance of either male or female genitals." For this law a minor is an unmarried person under 18, and each sale is a Class I misdemeanor.

Novelties that aren't genital-shaped aren't clearly covered, and Nebraska doesn't ban selling sexual devices to adults. The statute has no license, record-keeping or screening requirement. But displaying for sale to a minor is itself an offense, so keep adult items out of view and behind the counter.

Two less obvious points. Selling legally obscene material is a crime even to adults, though ordinary commercial adult media is rarely prosecuted. And a minor may not be employed to assist in selling obscene material unless a parent or guardian gives a written affidavit first, which matters if anyone under 18 works your register.

**Answer, crossbows:** No Nebraska or federal law sets a minimum age to buy one. A crossbow isn't a "firearm" under Nebraska law, so the ban on transferring firearms to juveniles doesn't apply. Game and Parks rules on crossbows govern hunting, not buying. I found no record-keeping duty. Your 18+ rule is a lawful store policy.

**Other items to keep off the floor unless licensed:** tobacco and vapes (age 21, retail license required), kratom (age 21), fireworks (licensed seasonal sales only).

**Basis:**

- [Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 28-807 to 28-813](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/display_html.php?begin_section=28-807&end_section=28-829): § 28-807 (definitions, minor is under 18 and unmarried), § 28-808 (sale or display to minors), § 28-809 (exhibition), § 28-811(3) (employing minors), § 28-813 (obscenity).
- § 28-1201 ("firearm" means a weapon that fires by explosive) and § 28-1204.01 (transfer of a firearm to a juvenile).
- Tobacco and vapor products: §§ 28-1419, 28-1420. Kratom: LB 230 (2025).

**Confidence:** High on the state adult-item statutes. Medium on crossbows, since it's a finding that no law exists. Low on Omaha, whose code I couldn't open.

**Verify:** Omaha Municipal Code Chapter 20 (weapons, sales to minors) and Chapter 55 (zoning definitions for adult uses, including any share-of-stock threshold that would make the store an adult business). The codified section and penalty for the kratom law.

### 10. A returned adult item with a broken seal

**Answer:** You may throw it away. No Nebraska or Omaha rule on reselling or disposing of used sexual devices turned up. Since 18+ items are final sale, the clean answer is to refuse the return. If one comes back anyway, discard it where it can't be resold or reached by a minor, and never put it back on the floor or donate it.

**Basis:** No statute found. § 28-808 is the reason to keep a discarded item out of reach.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Verify:** Douglas County Health Department rules, if any.

### 11. Relying on the 18+ flag when someone else uses the card

**Answer:** The flag plus a real photo comparison very likely meets the standard. Neither the offense nor the defense requires an ID check at each sale. The defense asks whether you had "reasonable cause to believe" the buyer was 18 or older, "based on but not limited to" an official ID.

The weak point is the photo. The defense holds only if staff actually compare the face on screen to the person at the counter. "Knowingly" includes having "reason to know," so a buyer who looks young or doesn't match the photo needs an ID check, flag or not.

Build this into the register: an age-restricted sale requires the cashier to confirm the photo match, logged with cashier and time. Keep the signup record of who checked the ID and when, because the defense is yours to prove.

**Basis:** § 28-807(5) ("knowingly"); [§ 28-810(1)](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=28-810&print=true) (reasonable-cause defense).

**Confidence:** Medium. No case law was checked.

**Verify:** That no Nebraska case requires an ID check at the time of each sale for this defense.

## E. Returns and store credit

No law requires a return policy. The two real problems: the member return promise as written is probably a federal "written warranty," and letting store credit expire doesn't let you keep the money.

### 12. Return policy requirements and enforceability

**Answer:** Neither Nebraska nor federal law requires a return policy, and nothing bars store-credit-only returns or the 5% haircut. Your wording is enforceable if customers see it before they pay: signs at the register and on the floor, then the receipt. A receipt alone can come too late.

Two bigger problems sit underneath:

1. **"All sales final" doesn't disclaim warranties.** As a merchant, you give an implied warranty that goods, used ones included, are fit for ordinary use given their type and price. Only a conspicuous "as is" or a disclaimer that names merchantability, shown before the sale, removes it. Without one, a guest with a dead item still has a warranty claim, whatever the return sign says.
2. **The member promise is probably a "written warranty" under Magnuson-Moss.** A written promise of a remedy if the product fails a stated standard ("main function doesn't work") within a stated time fits the definition. Once you give one, you can't disclaim implied warranties on that item. You can only limit their length to the warranty's 3 days, in clear language, under a "Limited Warranty" label with the required disclosures.

FTC rules exempt policies that allow returns "for any reason" for credit. Yours doesn't qualify, because it's tied to a defect.

Pick one fix:

- **Option A (my pick):** make the member return no-fault. "Members may return eligible items within 3 days, for any reason, for 95% store credit." It stops being a warranty, so a store-wide "AS IS, NO WARRANTIES" disclaimer works on everything. The cost is more returns.
- **Option B:** keep the defect condition and comply. Title it "Limited Warranty," limit implied warranties to 3 days in clear language, add the FTC disclosures for items over $15, and make the terms available before sale.

Either way, show "AS IS" on signs, as-is tags and receipts, and never describe used goods as new.

**Basis:**

- Neb. U.C.C. [§ 2-314](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/ucc.php?code=2-314) (merchantability), [§ 2-316(2) and (3)(a)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/ucc.php?code=2-316) (disclaimers), [§ 2-719](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/ucc.php?code=2-719) (limited remedies, failure of essential purpose), § 1-201(b)(10) (conspicuous).
- 15 U.S.C. §§ 2301(6)(B), 2303, 2308(a) to (c), 2310(d); [16 CFR 700.3(b), 700.5, 700.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-700), Parts 701 and 702; [FTC, Businessperson's Guide to Federal Warranty Law](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/businesspersons-guide-federal-warranty-law).
- § 87-302(a)(7) (used goods represented as new); § 59-1602.

**Confidence:** High that no policy is required. Medium-high that the member promise is a written warranty; no case is on point.

**Verify:** The Magnuson-Moss classification and which option to take. Whether 3 days is a "reasonable duration" under Option B. Whether a 95% credit still fits the FTC's "for any reason" exemption under Option A.

### 13. Store credit: expiration, fees, unclaimed property

**Answer:** Nebraska allows expiration dates and fees on gift certificates and gift cards, but both must be printed clearly on the certificate or card. The catch is unclaimed property: an expiration date doesn't let you keep the money.

Under Nebraska's unclaimed property act:

- A gift certificate or card with no fees and no expiration date is never presumed abandoned.
- One with an expiration date or fees is presumed abandoned 3 years after issue, and its full unused value goes to the State Treasurer.
- A "credit memo" unredeemed for 3 years is presumed abandoned, with no exemption for credit that never expires.
- Reports are due by Nov 1 for the year ending June 30. Holders with $50 or less in a year report once the total passes $50.

So adding an expiration date turns a balance you might never report into one you must report at 3 years. Return store credit may count as a "credit memo" either way, and that classification is the open question. Free rewards aren't mentioned in the act at all.

The federal gift card rules (no expiry under 5 years, tight limits on inactivity fees) probably don't reach your return store credit. CFPB commentary excludes store credit issued for returns when refunds by card aren't advertised to the public. Loyalty rewards are excluded only if the card or code says "Reward" or similar, and states the expiration date, on its front. A balance on a membership card or phone lookup can count as a "card, code, or other device."

Banked rewards are the soft spot. A member who skips a discount to bank 105% has arguably paid for stored value. If so, the balance is a store gift card: no expiry under 5 years, and no fees except after a year of inactivity.

My recommendation: no fees on anything, and store credit and banked rewards never expire. Instant rewards are used the moment they're given, so expiry doesn't arise; if the monthly cover sits as a balance, treat it like banked rewards. If you still want expiry on rewards, print "Reward" and the expiry rule on the card front and show each date on receipts and in the app. Skip tiering. It multiplies the dates you must disclose for very little gain.

**Basis:**

- [Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 69-1305.02, 69-1305.03, 69-1308, 69-1310](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/display_html.php?begin_section=69-1301&end_section=69-1332) (credit memos, gift certificates, catch-all, reporting).
- 15 U.S.C. § 1693l-1; [12 CFR 1005.20(a)(2), (a)(4), (b), (d), (e)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/) and the [Official Interpretation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/interp-20/), comments 20(a)-1, 20(a)(4)-2, 20(b)(4)-2.ii and 20(b)(4)-2.v.

**Confidence:** High on the statute and rule text. Low on how Nebraska classifies return store credit and rewards.

**Verify:** With the State Treasurer's Unclaimed Property Division: whether return store credit is a "credit memo" or a "gift card," and whether free or banked rewards are reportable. With counsel: whether banked rewards are "issued in exchange for payment" under Reg E.

### 14. Does "no cash value" change anything?

**Answer:** No, not legally. Whether a balance is a gift card, credit memo or loyalty reward turns on how it was issued and what it can buy, not on the label. The phrase does confirm you won't redeem for cash, and Nebraska has no law requiring cash-out of small balances.

Two side points work in your favor. Value usable only at Eco-Thrift is outside Nebraska's money transmitter law and FinCEN's prepaid access rule (closed loop, under $2,000 a day). For accounting, unused store credit and banked rewards are liabilities until spent, and your CPA should book them that way.

**Basis:** Neb. Rev. Stat. § 8-2702 (closed-loop and loyalty-program value excluded from "stored value"); 31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(4); § 69-1305.03 (no cash-out requirement); ASC 606-10-55-46 to 55-49 (breakage).

**Confidence:** High.

**Verify:** Nothing urgent.

## F. Sales tax

Your "tax on what's actually paid" rule is right for rewards and wrong for store credit and returns. Omaha's combined rate for October 2026 is 7.0% (5.5% state, 1.5% city), per DOR's local rate table effective July 1, 2026. That assumes the store is inside Omaha city limits and outside the Avenue One Good Life District, which has special tax rules.

| Item | Treat as | Tax is charged on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instant member reward | Discount | Price after the reward |
| Banked reward, including the 5% bonus | Discount on the later sale | Later price after the reward |
| Monthly $10 cover | Discount | Price after the cover |
| Store credit spent on a purchase | Payment, like a gift certificate | Full price |
| Return paid as store credit | Refund | Credit back the tax on the 95% |

### 15. Is a reward at the register a discount or a payment?

**Answer:** A discount. A store-funded price cut that no third party reimburses comes off the sales price before tax. DOR treats unreimbursed coupons, certificates and vouchers the same way. Manufacturer coupons and other third-party-reimbursed amounts don't reduce the taxable price.

Stop calling it an "instant rebate." DOR's regulation says cash and instant rebates are not deductible. It defines an instant rebate as one the manufacturer reimburses, so yours isn't, but the label invites an auditor to disallow it. Call it a "member discount" and show it as its own line before tax.

The $10 monthly cover works the same way. Don't market it with words suggesting you cover the customer's tax: Nebraska bars retailers from advertising that they'll pay or absorb the tax.

**Basis:** [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 77-2701.35(3)(a)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=77-2701.35) (discounts not reimbursed by a third party are excluded from sales price); [Reg-1-024.01 to 024.03](https://revenue.nebraska.gov/sites/revenue.nebraska.gov/files/doc/legal/regs/1-024.pdf) (discounts and rebates); [Reg-1-037.03 and 037.04](https://revenue.nebraska.gov/sites/revenue.nebraska.gov/files/doc/legal/regs/1-037.pdf) (coupons and certificates); § 77-2703(1)(b) and DOR guide 6-493 (no advertising that the retailer absorbs tax).

**Confidence:** High.

### 16. A banked reward spent later

**Answer:** Most likely a discount on the later sale, so tax is on the price after the reward, which is what you do now. Taxing the full $100 on the sale where the reward is banked is also correct.

The reasoning: the statute doesn't require a discount to arise on the same sale, the reward is store-funded, and no third party reimburses it. New York, Tennessee and California treat store-funded loyalty rewards this way. Nebraska has no ruling on point.

The label matters only as evidence; substance controls. "Credit" reads like a payment, and DOR treats gift and promotional certificates as cash when redeemed. So call it a reward, record it as a discount line rather than a tender type, and keep it non-transferable with no cash-out.

The tax stakes here are small. Even if DOR treated banked rewards as certificates, the combined tax base of the two sales shifts by about the 5% bonus, a few cents per reward.

**Basis:** § 77-2701.35(3)(a); Reg-1-037.04, 037.05 and 037.05A.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Verify:** Whether to ask DOR in writing before launch.

### 17. The 5% banking bonus

**Answer:** No change. The bonus is more store-funded value the member didn't pay for, so the full 105% reduces the later sale's price. Apply it only to taxable items, and never let it push an item below zero.

**Basis:** Reg-1-024.01; Reg-1-037.04.

**Confidence:** Medium.

### 18. Tax on a return paid as store credit

**Answer:** Refund or credit the tax on the part you give back. This is the one place the current design is clearly wrong.

|  | Price | Tax at 7% | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original sale | $100.00 | $7.00 | $107.00 |
| Store credit issued | $95.00 | $6.65 | $101.65 |
| Kept by the store | $5.00 | $0.35 | $5.35 |

Deduct the $95 from gross sales on the return for the period you issue the credit. The $5 you keep works like a restocking fee and stays taxable.

Store credit then works like cash. A later purchase paid with it is taxed on the full price. Your current rule taxes only the non-credit portion, which under-collects, because the original sale was already backed out of your taxable sales.

Define "price paid" for returns as the sales price after member discounts, including any part paid with store credit.

**Basis:** [Reg-1-025.01 and 025.03](https://revenue.nebraska.gov/sites/revenue.nebraska.gov/files/doc/legal/regs/1-025.pdf) (refund or credit the tax; restocking fees stay taxable; deduct in the period credited); § 77-2701.35(1) (consideration includes "cash, credit, property"); Reg-1-037.05A (certificates redeemed as cash).

**Confidence:** High on the rule. Medium that DOR treats return store credit like a gift certificate, which no Nebraska guidance names directly.

**Verify:** Which Form 10 line the return deduction belongs on.

### 19. Records for an audit

**Answer:** Keep records at least 3 years, and 6 is safer, because DOR can assess back 6 years when a return wasn't filed or understated tax by more than 25%. Discounts must appear on your records and be substantiated.

- **Per sale:** date, items, tag price, each discount by type (member discount, banked reward, bonus, monthly cover), net taxable price, tax, and each tender (cash, card, store credit).
- **Per member:** a rewards ledger of amounts earned, banked, bonused and used.
- **Per store credit:** a ledger tying each credit to its original sale, with the tax credited, the 5% kept, and every redemption.
- **Program level:** the terms showing rewards are store-funded, return records, and a monthly reconciliation of POS totals to each Form 10.

**Basis:** Neb. Rev. Stat. § 77-2711 (records, at least 3 years); [Reg-1-008](https://revenue.nebraska.gov/sites/revenue.nebraska.gov/files/doc/legal/regs/1-008.pdf) (records); § 77-2709 (3-year and 6-year assessment periods); Reg-1-024.01 (discounts must be substantiated).

**Confidence:** High.

## G. Program design risk

Member pricing against a real tag price is lawful. The slogan needs one qualifier, and the pricing method needs to be disclosed up front.

### 20. Advertising "the longer it waits, the less you pay"

**Answer:** The structure is fine; the slogan on its own isn't. The tag price is a real price guests pay every day, which is what the FTC requires of a comparison price. Member pricing is a bargain conditioned on joining, so the FTC expects every condition to be clear at the outset.

Unqualified, the slogan tells guests their price drops over time, which is false for them. Nebraska's deceptive trade practices act covers advertising a price "in any manner calculated or tending to mislead." The fix is two words: "Thrift+ members: the longer it waits, the less you pay."

The daily, app-only member price is lawful. Nebraska has no item-pricing law, though the state can check that registers charge the prices you represent. Three habits keep it safe:

- Publish the reward schedule in the store and in the terms, so a member can predict their price.
- Show the member price before payment and charge exactly that.
- Never raise tag prices to fund the discount, and don't advertise "savings" off tags that almost nobody pays.

Exposure is AG civil penalties up to $2,000 per violation, plus private suits for actual damages and attorney fees.

**Basis:** [16 CFR 233.1, 233.4(b) and (c), 233.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-233) (FTC pricing guides); [Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-302(a)(10) and (12)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=87-302) (misleading price advertising; price reductions); §§ 87-303, 87-303.11 (remedies); §§ 59-1602, 59-1609, 59-1614 (Consumer Protection Act); § 89-187 (price verification).

**Confidence:** High on the slogan fix. Medium on the rest.

**Verify:** What LB 838 (2026) changes in § 87-302, operative Jan 1, 2027.

### 21. Free membership with ID required only for 18+ items and returns

**Answer:** Lawful, and sensible. The ID rule is neutral, tied to real legal reasons, and customers without ID still get a card. The risks sit at the edges:

- **ID types:** accept any government photo ID, including foreign passports and consular IDs, and never ask about citizenship. That avoids a national-origin problem under the public accommodations laws.
- **Photo:** some people object to being photographed on religious grounds, and some disabilities make it hard. Make the photo optional; members without one show ID at each restricted sale or return.
- **Accessibility:** staff should be able to complete signup for anyone who can't use the screen, and any member app should be accessible.
- **Children:** set a minimum signup age of 13. In-store signup is outside COPPA, but an app that collects a child's phone number or login online is not. 18-year-olds can sign binding contracts in Nebraska; younger members' contracts are voidable.
- **"Free":** say at signup what data you collect and why.

**Basis:** [Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 20-132 to 20-143](https://neoc.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/PA%20Law%20full%20page%208-25.pdf) (public accommodations: race, color, sex, religion, national origin, disability, ancestry, and other classes); Omaha's human rights ordinance (adds sexual orientation and gender identity; not opened); 42 U.S.C. § 12182(b)(2)(A) (ADA); 15 U.S.C. § 6501 and [16 CFR Part 312](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule) (COPPA, as amended in 2025); § 43-2101; 16 CFR 251.1(c) (use of "free").

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

**Verify:** Omaha's protected classes and complaint procedure. Whether to require a parent's consent for members under 18.
