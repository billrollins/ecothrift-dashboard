# Thrift+ Limited Warranty Review

Eco-Thrift LLC, Omaha · Oct 1, 2026 · @Bill Rollins

Short-form research for attorney review, not legal advice. Each answer gives the call, the basis, confidence, and what to verify.

## Answers A to D

### A. Is it a warranty at all

**1. Written warranty?** Yes. A written promise to "refund, repair, replace, or take other remedial action" if a product fails a stated standard is a written warranty. Store credit counts as "other remedial action," and the 3-day period supplies the required time limit. Credit instead of cash doesn't change that. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2301(6)(B); 16 CFR 700.3(a). *Confidence:* High.

**2. Is 90% store credit a real remedy?** Yes. The Act sets minimum remedy standards only for Full warranties, so a Limited warranty can offer partial store credit. It becomes a problem only if the credit can't actually be used (see Q17). *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2304 (Full warranties only). *Confidence:* High.

**3. Can wording avoid warranty status?** No. Substance controls, not the label. The FTC exempts only returns allowed "for any reason" and unconditional satisfaction guarantees. A defect-based return called a "return policy" is still a warranty, just a noncompliant one, and your disclaimers would fail. The labeled Limited Warranty is the safer route. *Basis:* 16 CFR 700.3(b), 700.5. *Confidence:* High.

### B. Required form and content

**4. Title.** "Limited Warranty" is required for products over $10, shown as a caption or prominent title separate from the text. "Thrift+ Limited Warranty" works. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2303(a); 16 CFR 700.6. *Confidence:* High.

**5. Required contents for items over $15.** The draft has items 1, 3, 7 and 9, but 2 and 4 need tightening and 5 is missing.

Required sentences, verbatim:

- (7): "Some States do not allow limitations on how long an implied warranty lasts, so the above limitation may not apply to you."
- (8): "Some States do not allow the exclusion or limitation of incidental or consequential damages, so the above limitation or exclusion may not apply to you."
- (9): "This warranty gives you specific legal rights, and you may also have other rights which vary from State to State."

You may combine (7) and (8) into one sentence. *Basis:* 16 CFR 701.2, 701.3(a) and (b). *Confidence:* High.

**6. Items $15 or less.** The disclosure rules (Part 701) and pre-sale rules (Part 702) don't apply. The "Limited Warranty" title applies above $10. The ban on disclaiming implied warranties applies at any price once you give a written warranty. One document can cover every price, and that's simplest. *Basis:* 16 CFR 701.2, 702.1; 15 U.S.C. §§ 2303(a), 2308. *Confidence:* High.

**7. Name, address, start date, claim steps.** Yes. Name Eco-Thrift LLC with a mailing address or phone, and give step-by-step claim instructions. A start date is required only if coverage starts other than at purchase, but spell out how the 3 days are counted anyway. *Basis:* 16 CFR 701.3(a)(4), (5). *Confidence:* High.

**8. Single document; plain language.** Yes, all nine items in one document: the poster. The receipt can only point to it. Part 701 doesn't define "clear and conspicuous." In practice: large high-contrast type, short sentences, no fine print, and the title set apart. *Basis:* 16 CFR 701.3(a). *Confidence:* High.

### C. Making it available before the sale

**9. Seller-warrantor duties.** For items over $15, choose one: show the text in close proximity to each warranted product, or furnish it on request before sale with prominent signs saying it's available. With coverage store-wide, use the second: the poster at each register, copies at the counter, and floor signs saying "Ask for the Thrift+ Limited Warranty." The receipt doesn't count because it arrives after the sale. *Basis:* 16 CFR 702.3(a). *Confidence:* Medium-high. *Verify:* Whether a register poster alone also satisfies the "close proximity" option.

**10. App and website.** Helpful, not required, and not a substitute for in-store availability. Posting it online lets staff show it on a screen when asked. *Basis:* 16 CFR 702.3(a), (b)(2). *Confidence:* Medium.

### D. Limiting and disclaiming implied warranties

**11. Limiting implied warranties to 3 days.** Allowed in principle, but 3 days is the most attackable term in the program. The limit must be "of reasonable duration" and "conscionable," and no case sets a floor for used goods. It fails if a typical defect can't show up within 3 days, or if a court sees 3 days as built to defeat claims. Going to 7 days would cost little and cut the risk a lot. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2308(b). *Confidence:* Low to medium. *Verify:* Whether counsel is comfortable with 3 days.

**12. Required language and placement.** The limit must appear on the face of the warranty itself (the poster), in clear and unmistakable words, prominently displayed. Use bold type near the remedy. Suggested: "Any implied warranty, including merchantability and fitness for a particular purpose, lasts only 3 days from the day of sale." Then the required sentence from Q5. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2308(b); 16 CFR 701.3(a)(7). *Confidence:* High.

**13. Member purchases of excluded items.** You can fully disclaim them, because no written warranty was given on those products. But exclusion alone doesn't disclaim: without an AS IS notice, an excluded item still carries the full implied warranty. Store-wide AS IS signs cover this, as long as a member can tell before paying which items are excluded. Plain price tags can stay plain: put a NO THRIFT+ WARRANTY sticker only on the specific items you sell as-is or for parts, and post signs in the clothing, 18+ and crossbow areas. Don't exclude "untested" items: if most stock is untested, that exclusion would swallow the warranty. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2308(a) ("with respect to such consumer product"); Neb. U.C.C. § 2-316(3)(a). *Confidence:* Medium.

**14. AS IS for guests.** "As is" excludes all implied warranties. It has to be conspicuous and seen before payment. A large sign at each register, where payment happens, is likely enough; add the entrance and the receipt for backup. A receipt alone is too late. "Conspicuous" means a reasonable person ought to have noticed it: caps, bold, larger or contrasting type. *Basis:* [Neb. U.C.C. § 2-316(3)(a)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/ucc.php?code=2-316); § 1-201(b)(10). *Confidence:* Medium-high.

**15. Same item, AS IS for guests and warranty for members.** Fine. Each sale is its own contract. Avoid conflicting signs by having the AS IS sign name the exception (see the sign text below), and keep the guest line off the warranty poster. *Confidence:* Medium-high.

**16. Used Car Rule and other used-goods rules.** The Used Car Rule covers only used vehicle dealers. No general federal used-goods warranty rule exists. Nebraska's deceptive trade practices act bars passing off used goods as new. *Basis:* 16 CFR Part 455; Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-302(a)(7). *Confidence:* High. *Verify:* Whether Omaha's secondhand-dealer ordinance applies to your inventory sources (a licensing question, not a warranty one).

## Answers E to H

### E. The remedy and its limits

**17. Store credit as the exclusive remedy.** Yes, if the text says it's exclusive. "That is the only remedy" probably works; "sole and exclusive remedy" removes doubt. It fails of its essential purpose when the member can't get the benefit: the store closes, the member is banned, or staff refuse valid claims. Add a fallback: if store credit can't be used, refund 90% in cash. *Basis:* [Neb. U.C.C. § 2-719(1)(b), (2)](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/ucc.php?code=2-719). *Confidence:* Medium-high.

**18. Excluding incidental and consequential damages.** Yes, unless unconscionable. Limiting damages for personal injury from consumer goods is presumed unconscionable, so carve it out. Wording: "We are not responsible for incidental or consequential damages, such as damage to other property or travel costs. This does not limit claims for personal injury." Then the combined sentence: "Some States do not allow limitations on how long an implied warranty lasts, or the exclusion or limitation of incidental or consequential damages, so the above limitations or exclusions may not apply to you." *Basis:* Neb. U.C.C. § 2-719(3); 16 CFR 701.3(a)(8), (b). *Confidence:* High.

**19. Keeping 10%.** Lawful for a Limited warranty, and 10% is a defensible level; 15% is about the ceiling, and 25% invites a finding that the remedy fails or is unconscionable. Describe it as the amount of the credit ("store credit for 90% of what you paid"), not as a restocking charge. A "charge" on a defective item reads worse to customers and invites a fight over whether it's conscionable. The sales tax result is the same either way. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2304 (no-charge rule applies to Full warranties only). *Confidence:* Medium-high.

**20. Sales tax on the returned amount.** Yes, credit the tax on the 90%. On a $100 item at 7%, that's $90 plus $6.30. The warranty doesn't strictly have to say so, but "90% of the price, before tax" reads as if tax is kept. Say "plus the sales tax on that amount." *Basis:* [Neb. Reg-1-025.01](https://revenue.nebraska.gov/sites/revenue.nebraska.gov/files/doc/legal/regs/1-025.pdf); 16 CFR 701.3(a)(3). *Confidence:* High.

### F. Who decides, and disputes

**21. "Main function does not work."** Workable, but define it with examples. The rule requires a clear description of what's covered. Suggested: "It does the main thing it's made for: a lamp lights, a blender blends, a toaster heats." Let a member ask a manager to review a denial. Don't add "in our sole discretion"; it invites an argument that the promise is illusory. *Basis:* 16 CFR 701.3(a)(2). *Confidence:* Medium.

**22. Requiring the card and your records.** Yes. You may limit coverage to the member who bought the item, require proof from your sales records, and refuse items not bought on the card, as long as the poster says so. Accept a phone-number lookup when the card is lost, since the record is what proves the sale. *Basis:* 16 CFR 701.3(a)(1), (5). *Confidence:* High.

**23. Informal dispute procedure.** Optional. Part 703 applies only if you require members to use one before suing. Skip it and leave it off the poster. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2310(a)(3); 16 CFR 703.1. *Confidence:* High.

**24. Exposure.** A member can sue for damages in state court; Nebraska small claims takes cases up to $7,500 as of July 1, 2025. A consumer who wins may recover costs and attorney fees, which is the real multiplier on a $20 item. Federal court requires $50,000 in total or a class with 100 named plaintiffs. A class suit must first give you a chance to cure. The FTC can also enforce, and Nebraska's consumer protection acts add AG penalties. The realistic risk is a store-wide defect, such as void disclaimers, multiplied across many sales. *Basis:* [15 U.S.C. § 2310(b), (d), (e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2310); Neb. Rev. Stat. § 25-2802 ([limit raised by LB 139 (2024)](https://nebraskajudicial.gov/administration/news/nebraska-increases-jurisdictional-limits-small-claims-and-county-courts)). *Confidence:* High.

### G. Is it insurance or a service contract

**25. Service contract or insurance?** Neither, as long as it's free. A warranty is part of the bargain at the sale with no charge beyond the price; a service contract costs extra or is bought later. A seller standing behind its own goods at no charge isn't insurance. *Basis:* 15 U.S.C. § 2301(8); 16 CFR 700.11. *Confidence:* Medium-high.

**26. The $10 monthly "cover."** Yes, this is the soft spot. Saying the first $10 of rewards each month is "applied as a cover for the card" means the member pays $10 a month in rewards to stay a member. That cuts against "free" membership, gives a service-contract or insurance argument something to grab, and looks like a monthly fee taken from a reward balance (Q13 of the earlier memo). The fix: take nothing for membership. If the $10 is a perk, describe it as a monthly reward given to the member. Say on the poster: "Included with covered purchases at no extra charge." *Basis:* 16 CFR 700.11 ("consideration in addition to the purchase price"); 16 CFR 251.1 (use of "free"). *Confidence:* Medium. *Depends on:* What the cover actually does in the system. If nothing is ever taken from the member, only the wording needs fixing.

**27. Nebraska service contract law.** The only Nebraska service contract statute I found covers motor vehicle service contracts (insurance backing and filing with the Department of Insurance). Nothing found for general consumer goods, and you aren't selling a contract anyway. *Basis:* Neb. Rev. Stat. § 44-3522 ([LB 188 (2007)](https://www.NebraskaLegislature.gov/FloorDocs/100/PDF/Slip/LB188.pdf)). *Confidence:* Medium. *Verify:* That no general service contract act exists.

### H. Changes over time

**28. Changing or ending it.** Yes, for future sales. No statute sets a notice period: post the new version before it takes effect. Each sale keeps the warranty in force on its sale date, because the warranty is part of that sale's bargain. Date each version, keep old ones, and print the version date on receipts. *Basis:* 16 CFR 700.11 (basis of the bargain at the sale). *Confidence:* High.

## Most likely to be challenged

Fix them in this order.

1. **Signs and tags that don't match the warranty.** Your "AS-IS No Returns" signs cover guests, but once members get returns they contradict the warranty, and a plain price tag doesn't tell a member whether an item is covered. A sale whose status is unclear can carry a full implied warranty, with up to 4 years to sue. Fix first: add the member exception to the signs and put a NO THRIFT+ WARRANTY sticker only on items sold as-is or for parts (Q13, Q14; Neb. U.C.C. §§ 2-316, 2-725).
2. **The 3-day implied warranty limit.** It must be a "reasonable duration" and conscionable, and 3 days for used goods is untested. If it fails, member items carry implied warranties of normal length. Fix: move to 7 days, or accept the risk knowingly (Q11).
3. **Incomplete warranty text.** The draft lacks the warrantor's name and contact, claim steps, a clear "exclusive" remedy and a definition of "main function," and its tax wording is wrong. Noncompliance can void your implied-warranty limit and opens the door to fee-shifting suits. Fix: use the poster above (Q5, Q17, Q20, Q21).

Also fix the $10 "cover" wording before launch, so membership is plainly free (Q26).
