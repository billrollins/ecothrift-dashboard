When we opened, the whole shop fit on three folding tables. Two years on, we have a back room, a steam press, and a wall of regulars who know our names.

Growth is a strange thing for a thrift store. The instinct is to add more, faster, louder. But the soul of this place was always in the slowness, in the hunt, in the conversation at the counter about where a coat had been before it found you.

Keeping the hunt human

So we made a rule early on. Every decision about scaling had to pass one test: does it keep the floor feeling like a discovery, or does it turn us into a warehouse with good lighting?

The best retail moment isn't the sale. It's the gasp when someone finds the thing they didn't know they were looking for.

That principle has cost us money more than once, and I would make the same choice every time.
