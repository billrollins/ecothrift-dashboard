# No trespassing: what Nebraska and Omaha require (for the back area sign, #114)

Bill (11-12): "State of NE / City of Omaha have specific requirement that will make homeless people not allowed on property, please try to find". Store: 8425 West Center Road, Omaha (inside the city; OPD Southwest Precinct on OPD's 2019 map).

## The short answer

1. **A plain "No Trespassing" sign has no required wording, size or colour** in state or city law. State law asks only for "Posting in a manner prescribed by law or reasonably likely to come to the attention of intruders" (Neb. Rev. Stat. 28-521(1)(b)). Omaha's trespass ordinance (OMC 20-154) has no posting rule at all. So a clear, well-placed sign at each way into the back is what the law asks; it makes a person who stays there "know" they are not allowed.
2. **The "specific requirement" Bill heard of is Omaha's Closed Property registration** (OMC 20-156.01 to 20-156.07, OPD Form 22). It is the one way police may remove and cite people on the property **when nobody from the store is there** (after hours): the store registers the back lot with OPD, appoints Omaha police officers as its agents, carries $100,000 of insurance naming the City, and posts **city-made signs** with wording set by ordinance:
   > "PROPERTY CLOSED to all persons and vehicles from _______ to _______ on ________. Violators will be prosecuted under Omaha City Ordinances §§ 20-156.01 through 20-156.07. Contract #_______" (OMC 20-156.05; the registration's expiration date must be on every sign)
   We do **not** design or print that sign: the City's Permits & Inspections picks the spots on site and the signs are ordered from the City's Traffic Maintenance Facility at our cost. **Displaying that wording before we are registered is itself unlawful** (20-156.05).
3. **During open hours** (or for one named person who keeps coming back), nothing is filed with OPD: a manager tells the person to leave (OMC 20-155; refusing is the offence and a Class II misdemeanor under 28-521(5)); for a repeat person the store writes its own **Ban and Bar** letter (OPD form; the store keeps it, OPD does not).
4. **Without registration, OPD's written policy for closed properties says no enforcement**: "If signs are posted but officers find either the registration guidelines have not been met or the registration is expired, no enforcement action shall be taken." Whether officers would still cite on a plain posted lot with no one from the store present is **not written anywhere I found**.

## So the plan for #114

- **Our sign now** (designed by us, legal as is): "NO TRESPASSING. Private property. Employees and deliveries only." plus "Violators will be prosecuted" is fine; do **not** cite 20-156 or say "property closed / registered" until the registration exists.
- **Decide (Bill, yes/no):** apply for Closed Property registration for the back lot (partial registration of only the back is allowed, 20-156.06). Needs: the original signed packet mailed to OPD; a $100,000 surety bond or a liability certificate naming "THE CITY OF OMAHA IS AN ADDITIONAL INSURED TO COMPLY WITH CITY ORDINANCE SEC. 20-156.04"; Chief of Police approval; a site visit by Permits & Inspections; city signs bought and installed by us; staff on the lot during closed hours must carry ID showing permission ("There is no exception"); renew every December 31. If the store does not have sole control of the lot (shared with other tenants), the others must sign too.
- Who to call: Business Watch Squad (402) 444-5772, businesswatch@cityofomaha.org (the web page); the form itself names the Neighborhood Services Unit (402) 444-3954. Same address: 505 S 15th St, Omaha, NE 68102. A Business Watch officer will do a free security audit on request.
