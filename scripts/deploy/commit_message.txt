feat(tars): rebuild the bench across the width of the page

Everything above the work log was a stack of full-width bars, each using a
fraction of its row. It now splits: the item on the left, the grades it
could reach on the right.

Left is the item as a whole — what it is, what it is worth, where its time
has gone, the current-grade claim, and the ways it can leave. The claim is
load-bearing, since every rate in the table is measured from it, so it
carries an amber edge until it is answered.

Right is one row per grade with the item itself on top. Work that informs
every grade at once — opening it up, looking things up — is not an outcome,
it belongs to the item, and giving it a row means the clock always has an
honest place to go.

Grades now sit in scale order and stay there. Sorting by rate meant the
table rearranged itself under the hand every time an estimate changed; a
row you are reaching for should be where it was a second ago. The winner is
marked where it stands, which costs one badge and keeps the layout
learnable. Badges moved out of the grade name into their own column, beside
the Work button that starts the clock.

Undo, because Work now opens an action on the first press and a misclick
costs a row. It deletes the action and hands its seconds back to the one
before, which is where the time belonged anyway — whoever misclicked was
still doing the previous thing. Nothing is lost: the job total does not
move, only the row the time is filed under.

Going back to the queue now asks why, with four common reasons as one press
each. An item that reappears with no explanation just gets picked up and
put down again by the next person. Hold and Done already asked.

Scanning left the Bench header. It is how an item gets onto a bench, so it
belongs where you go looking for one; with something already in hand the
field was only a way to lose it. On Home with a clear bench the cursor now
waits there, since scanning is the one thing left to do.

Parts moved off the disposition row into its own button with a count, since
it opens a drawer and is not a way for the item to leave.

Tabs are drawn as tabs in both places, joining the surface they open rather
than floating above it as buttons.
