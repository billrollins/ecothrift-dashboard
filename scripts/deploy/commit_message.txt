feat(tars): make actions the thing time belongs to

Time on the bench was attributed to a grade and a mode, which said where
it went but never what was done. This makes an action the unit: one piece
of work — inspect, test, repair, assemble or salvage — pointed either at a
single grade or at the item as a whole, with the seconds banked against it.

Three rules hold the log together:

  The clock is never homeless. Check-in opens an initial inspection on the
  item, so from the moment an item lands there is somewhere for time to go.

  A pause is not a new action. Coming back to the grade you were already on
  resumes that action; one repair does not become five because someone took
  a phone call. Turning to a different scope closes the old action and opens
  a new one, and an explicit New action button covers the case where the
  same grade genuinely holds a second piece of work.

  Nothing is left unsaid. An action must be described before its author can
  start another. The moment you move on is the moment you know what you did,
  so it is also the last honest moment to write it down. The buttons that
  would move on are disabled and say why, so the rule is seen before it is
  hit rather than after.

Pressing Work now starts immediately with an Inspect action and a blank
description rather than opening a form. The cost of that is paid at the
other end, by the gate above, which is the cheaper place to pay it.

Inspect is its own category, separate from Test: finding out what is wrong
is different work from verifying that something works. Scope still decides
attribution — the item as a whole is charged to the item, because one
teardown informs every grade at once — but nothing says "looking" any more;
the bench reads "item 12m · grades 34m".

Also closes the two remaining gaps at the edges of a shift. Clocking out
with the clock running now asks first, writes up the work and stops the
clock in one go, instead of pausing quietly server-side and mentioning it
afterwards. The idle prompt now names the time the screen went quiet, so
the question it asks can actually be answered.

Backend: RestorationAction with job.current_action, a service that owns the
three rules, three endpoints, and 35 tests covering attribution, the resume
rule and the description gate.

Frontend: Work and Log tabs on the bench with Work leading, since work is
what someone is standing there to do; the log is read when a question comes
up. Categories are colour-coded and correctable as the work reveals itself.
